Frequently asked questions.

What a commission claim is, what it costs, and how to do it for nothing instead.

What is a car finance commission claim?

It is about how the finance was arranged and how the person selling it was paid, rather than anything to do with the car. Your lender paid commission to the dealer or broker who arranged the agreement.

The FCA has named three arrangements in particular: discretionary commission arrangements, where the dealer could influence the interest rate you were given; high commission, where the commission was large relative to the amount borrowed; and tied lender arrangements, where the market appeared to be compared but the dealer was tied to one lender. A claim is about whether that was made clear to you at the time.

What does this cost?

The initial check is free. If a claim goes ahead, the partner law firm works on a no win no fee basis and charges 18% to 36% including VAT of any compensation recovered. If the claim is settled through litigation the maximum deduction is capped at 48% including VAT.

You can cancel without charge within 14 days of instruction. After 14 days the firm may charge for reasonable costs already incurred.

Will the check affect my credit score?

No. It is a soft search. It is not visible to lenders and it does not affect your credit score.

What if I do not have my paperwork?

You do not need it. A soft search across multiple credit reference agencies identifies motor finance agreements taken out between April 2007 and November 2024, including agreements taken out under a former name or at a previous address.

You do not need to know which finance company you used either. Most people remember the dealership rather than the lender behind the agreement.

Can I claim for free instead?

Yes. You can complain free of charge directly to your lender, to the Financial Ombudsman Service, or through the FCA’s motor finance redress scheme. Taking one of those routes is a perfectly reasonable decision and you keep all of anything recovered.

The three routes are set out in full on claiming for free.

How much could I get?

The FCA currently estimates that most individuals will potentially receive an average of £830 in compensation per agreement. That is the regulator’s figure, per agreement, and not a promise about yours. Actual compensation varies and is not guaranteed.

The search looks across every agreement it finds, not only the one you remember, and each one is assessed on its own facts. Because parts of the scheme are suspended, the timing of any payment is not yet settled.

Which lenders can I claim against?

Any lender that provided PCP or hire purchase finance between April 2007 and November 2024. It is not a fixed list, and you do not need to know who your lender was: the search looks at the agreements registered to you rather than at a list of firms.

We hold a page on each of the eight that come up most often: Black Horse, Volkswagen, BMW, Santander, Mercedes-Benz, Close Brothers, Toyota and Moneybarn. The full list is on lenders.

Who actually handles my claim?

Not The PCP Claims Company. We identify the agreements in your name and refer them to a partner law firm. That firm reviews each agreement, gathers the evidence, deals with the lender, and takes the claim to the Financial Ombudsman or to court if it needs to go there.

That firm pays us a referral fee under an ongoing marketing agreement. We are paid whether or not you would have done better going direct, which is why the free routes are named on this page.
